01 / THE PROBLEM
Fact-checkers label truth — but not harm
During the COVID-19 pandemic, social media platforms labelled vaccine misinformation with truth ratings. But a message can be false and low-harm (a harmless myth) or false and extremely harmful (driving medical decisions).

Research shows that people's hesitancy to share misinformation is driven not just by whether they believe it, but by their perception of the harm it could cause to others. Current systems miss this second dimension entirely.

02 / THE DESIGN
PANTH: Platform for Automated Nudging of Truth and Harm
PANTH combines human fact-checkers and AI language models to extract and display two independent scores for every piece of content before sharing:

Truth-O-Meter — how verified is the claim?
Harm-O-Meter — how much harm could sharing this cause, across personal health, psychological, and community dimensions?

Together these scores form a pre-share nudge — a "Stop, Think" moment grounded in the empirical finding that community harm perception is the strongest predictor of sharing hesitancy.

03 / THE EVIDENCE
Grounded in a longitudinal study of 1,219 participants
A two-wave survey exposed US adults to 10 COVID-19 vaccine misinformation scenarios. Key findings driving the PANTH design:

Community harm → hesitancy — the strongest direct harm pathway (H5 ✓)
Belief bypasses harm reasoning — direct belief-to-sharing shortcut (H2d ✓, p ≤ .001)
Verification reinforces bias — fact-checks strengthen existing beliefs via confirmation bias (H1 ✓)

PANTH's dual-score design is derived from each of these findings, not bolted on post-hoc. The Harm-O-Meter targets the community harm pathway; the pre-share prompt interrupts the belief shortcut.
Verification → Belief → Harm Perception → Probabilistic Hesitant Sharing (PHS)
